# Foreign Investors & Market Entrants: A Concise Guide to Entering Bangladesh

Bangladesh is genuinely open to foreign capital — most sectors permit full foreign ownership with no requirement for a local partner. But "open" doesn't mean simple. Foreign investors typically need to clear two parallel regulatory tracks — company incorporation and investment registration — before capital can actually move and operations can begin.

## Ownership Rules in Practice

Bangladesh generally follows a non-discrimination principle, and full (100%) foreign ownership is permitted in the majority of sectors. That said, two categories sit outside this default:

- **Reserved sectors** — a short list, primarily defense-related and a few strategically sensitive industries, closed to private and foreign investment entirely.
- **Controlled or restricted sectors** — including certain service categories (freight/cargo forwarding, shipping agencies, courier services, buying houses) and select industries like RMG, banking, and insurance, which require local ownership or specific ministerial clearance.

Most consumer, industrial, and technology-sector investments fall outside both categories and can proceed as wholly foreign-owned entities.

## The Two-Track Entry Process

**Track 1: Company incorporation (RJSC).** Foreign shareholders generally form a private limited company — the entity type available to full foreign ownership, since Bangladesh's newer One Person Company structure is currently reserved for local entrepreneurs. This requires name clearance, constitutional documents, and, for foreign capital, a temporary bank account and an Encashment Certificate confirming the capital was converted into BDT.

**Track 2: Investment registration (BIDA).** Once incorporated, the investment itself must be registered with the Bangladesh Investment Development Authority through its One-Stop Service portal. BIDA registration is generally required to open a branch or liaison office, establish a factory, incorporate a wholly foreign-owned or joint venture company, sponsor foreign employee work permits, and access industrial land in Special Economic Zones.

Both tracks typically add up to several weeks in total — incorporation itself often completes within one to two weeks including capital remittance, with BIDA registration adding roughly one to two further weeks depending on documentation quality.

## Practical Considerations Investors Often Underweight

**The foreign-to-local employee ratio.** Industrial operations are generally expected to maintain a limited ratio of foreign to local staff — this affects workforce planning from day one, not just at the work-permit stage.

**Repatriation isn't automatic — it's procedural.** Profits, dividends, and capital can be repatriated, but this depends on proper documentation at the investment stage (Encashment Certificates, BIDA registration, Bangladesh Bank reporting) being in place from the start, not assembled retroactively when an investor wants to remit funds.

**Sector classification affects more than ownership.** A sector landing in the "controlled" list can also affect financing, import/export registration, and licensing timelines — this is worth confirming early, before a market-entry plan is finalized around assumptions that may not hold for that specific sector.

## The Bottom Line

Bangladesh's market-entry rules are investor-friendly in principle, but the process runs through two separate authorities with their own documentation requirements and timelines. Investors who sequence RJSC incorporation and BIDA registration correctly from the outset tend to reach operational readiness in weeks; those who treat them as one step frequently don't.
