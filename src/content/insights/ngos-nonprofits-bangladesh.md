# NGOs & Non-Profits: Registration and Compliance in Bangladesh

Non-profit organisations in Bangladesh do not all follow one registration route. The correct pathway depends on the legal structure and, critically, whether the organisation receives or intends to receive foreign donations.

## The Main Registration Pathways

### NGO Affairs Bureau (NGOAB)
Organisations receiving foreign donations generally need registration and approvals under the foreign-donation framework. NGOAB registration is typically an additional layer rather than a substitute for the organisation’s base legal registration.

### Department of Social Services (DSS)
Local social-welfare organisations may register through DSS where that framework fits their activities and funding model.

### RJSC / Society / Non-Profit Company Structures
An organisation may also have a base legal identity through an RJSC-registered structure, a society, or another permitted non-profit form.

The key practical point is that **base entity registration and foreign-funding approval can be separate requirements**.

## What NGOAB Compliance Involves

For foreign-funded organisations, the process can include an application, governing documents, project information and verification through multiple government bodies. This can take longer than a standard business registration and should be built into the funding timeline.

Project approval and reporting are also important. Registration does not mean every future project or budget change is automatically approved.

## Ongoing Compliance

Registered organisations should maintain clear processes for:

- Project scope and budget approvals
- Regular and final project reporting
- Audited financial records
- Foreign-donation banking channels
- Donor-ready documentation
- TIN and VAT assessment where applicable

## Common Mistakes

**Assuming local registration is enough when foreign funding begins.** A new funding source can create a new regulatory requirement.

**Underestimating the approval timeline.** Multi-agency verification should be planned before funds are expected.

**Changing project scope or budget informally.** Significant changes may require fresh approval rather than notification after the fact.

## The Bottom Line

For a foreign-funded non-profit, the question is often not “which authority?” but “which authorities?” Build the entity-registration, NGOAB, project-approval and reporting requirements into one compliance plan from the beginning.
